01 — About

Vague problem in, working system out

Most of what I've built started badly defined: the business describes a problem in its own words, compliance has a constraint nobody wrote down, the vendor's system does almost the right thing. I ask the questions that sharpen it, then come back with something that runs. At Säkra VP that's how the client risk model happened — Python against SQL, deployed on Azure, released through a pipeline that tests before anything ships. It classifies tens of thousands of clients, and the business acts on the output directly.

I work mostly in the seams between systems: integrations, file deliveries, debugging that spans our data, the vendor's platform and a third party — rarely obvious which of the three is wrong. That's the part I've gotten genuinely good at, and it's most of what makes unfamiliar code tractable.

I built sharpa.se because I wanted it to exist, and there was nobody to hand the hard parts to. I use Claude and Codex daily — a large part of why one person can carry it alongside a full-time job.
