01 — About

AI, but the part that has to keep working

The interesting problem with AI is rarely the model. It is everything around it: what goes in, whether the output can be trusted, and what happens when it is wrong. At Säkra I built a production workflow where Azure-hosted LLMs read incoming documents and extract the data downstream processing acts on. Before it was trusted in production I built a rule-based baseline and compared the model output against it — the flow now runs automatically and has replaced manual document handling.

That instinct comes from research. At Karolinska University Hospital I built and trained deep learning models in PyTorch for classification of pulmonary disease, and the full pipeline around them — raw medical imaging data through preprocessing and training to validated results. Working with clinical data teaches you quickly that an accuracy number without a validation story is worth nothing.

Alongside the AI work I own systems: the company's client risk model from methodology to deployed Python, automated data pipelines, and a custody platform holding over SEK 10 billion in client assets. I use AI as a daily part of how I build — it is how one person can run sharpa.se end to end — and I want it to be a bigger part of my next role.
